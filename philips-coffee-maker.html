Executive summary

Philips held 13% of a 5-product coffee maker market and wanted to know whether adding a water filter, an auto-grinder or both would grow its share. Using a choice-based conjoint study (185 respondents, 1,480 choices), we found 2 consumer segments with nearly opposite preferences. The larger segment (74%), feature-seeking value buyers, wants a 15-cup machine with an auto-grinder at $59, has no use for a water filter, and leans toward the Philips brand. We recommended building exactly that product. It is projected to lift Philips' overall share from 13% to 52%, drawing mostly from Cuisinart's $99 grinder model, which falls from 52% to 18%.

Objective

Determine whether a water filter or auto-grinder would grow Philips' share, design the best new product, and predict its effect on competing products.

Data and approach

The study used 16 product profiles from a fractional factorial design across 5 attributes:

  • Brand: Philips, Krups or Cuisinart
  • Capacity: 5, 10 or 15 cups
  • Price: $59, $79 or $99
  • Water filter: yes or no
  • Auto-grinder: yes or no

Each respondent chose from 8 sets of 3 profiles. We estimated mixture multinomial logit models on the effect-coded attributes in GLIMMIX, and BIC selected 2 segments. We used each segment's part-worths to identify its ideal product and to simulate market shares against the 5 existing products. We also visualized each segment's ideal product with ChatGPT and Gemini.

Key findings

  • Feature-seeking value buyers (74%). The auto-grinder is the strongest preference in the study (+1.12). They prefer a 15-cup capacity (+0.49) and the $59 price (+0.19), reject a water filter (-0.85), and are the only segment with a significant Philips brand preference (+0.18).
  • Simplicity seekers (26%). They prefer a 5-cup machine (+0.41) at $79 (+0.57), reject an auto-grinder (-0.70), and show no significant brand or filter preference.
  • The answer to Philips' question. Add the grinder and skip the filter. Neither segment values a filter.
  • Where share is today. Cuisinart's 10-cup, $99 grinder model held 52% of the market as the only product with a grinder.

Recommendations

  1. Launch a 15-cup Philips coffee maker with an auto-grinder and no water filter, priced at $59 and aimed at the larger segment. Projected overall share rises from 13% to 52%.
  2. Use the $59 price and the segment's existing Philips preference to compete directly against Cuisinart's $99 grinder model.

My role

Worked across every stage: model estimation and segment selection in GLIMMIX, interpreting segment preferences, the market share simulations, and writing the recommendations.

Limitations and what I'd do next

  • Market share is not profit. There was no cost data, and a $59 machine with a grinder may not be profitable. Next, I would simulate profit using unit costs.
  • In the larger segment, price preferences don't run in order: $99 is rated above $79. This suggests buyers may read price as a signal of quality, or that the price effect is weak. Testing more price levels would clarify it.
  • The results reflect stated choices in a survey, not actual purchases, and there were no demographics for targeting either segment.
