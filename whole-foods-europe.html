Executive summary

Whole Foods, positioned on service and store experience rather than price, was weighing entry into Europe. Using a European Commission survey of 1,669 shoppers in 105 regions across 7 countries, we grouped regions, not countries, by what drives shoppers' image of their grocery store. A 2-segment mixture regression revealed a service- and atmosphere-driven segment covering 69% of regions and a smaller price-driven segment. The segments cut across national borders. We recommended targeting the service- and atmosphere-driven segment, entering through eastern Germany and France and northern Spain and Italy, positioned on service quality and a localized atmosphere.

Objective

Identify which European regions best match Whole Foods' positioning, and recommend where to enter, how to position and how to roll out.

Data and approach

Shoppers rated their main grocery store on overall image and on 3 drivers (service, atmosphere and price) using 7-point scales. In R, we found store image correlated strongly with service (0.75), atmosphere (0.72) and price (0.67). We then estimated mixture regressions of store image on the 3 drivers in GLIMMIX, testing 1-6 segments. BIC was lowest at 2 segments. Because mixture models can settle on different solutions, we re-estimated the 2-segment model 6 times and kept the best fit, which explained 67% of the variation with clearly separated segments. Each region was then assigned to its most likely segment and mapped.

Key findings

  • Service- and atmosphere-driven segment (69% of regions). Service and atmosphere shape store image most. This segment weights atmosphere nearly 2x and service 1.3x as heavily as the price-driven segment.
  • Price-driven segment (31% of regions). Price perception is the strongest driver, with 1.2x the price sensitivity of the other segment. Atmosphere matters least.
  • Segments ignore borders. Spain, Italy and Germany each split across both segments, so entry decisions need to be made region by region.

Recommendations

  1. Target the service- and atmosphere-driven segment, which matches Whole Foods' positioning.
  2. Enter where that segment concentrates: eastern Germany and France, and northern Spain and Italy.
  3. Position on service quality and a localized atmosphere, investing in hiring and training and partnering with local businesses.

My role

Worked across every stage: data exploration and correlation analysis in R, mixture model estimation and selection in GLIMMIX, mapping regions to segments, and writing the recommendations.

Limitations and what I'd do next

  • Later reanalysisGrocers typically expand into one concentrated area first to keep distribution efficient, but our recommendation spans 4 countries at once. A tighter first phase would be eastern Germany, where neighboring regions (Brandenburg, Dresden, Halle and Thuringia) each have an 89-99% probability of belonging to the service-driven segment, with expansion outward from there.
  • Service and atmosphere ratings are highly correlated (0.77), which makes their separate effects harder to tease apart.
  • The survey covers shoppers' current stores, not Whole Foods itself, and some regions have few respondents. A real entry decision would also need market size, income, competition and real estate costs.
